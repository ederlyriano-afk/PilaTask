# What this is and isn't
My first ever website: [PilaTask - Project](https://pilatask.vercel.app/).
It's a simple practice website with the purpose of showcasing the PilaTask app idea and recruiting beta testers. 

Moving forward, the purpose of this README  file is not only about the skills and process, but to share my journey to anyone who needs it. I'm not an expert.

But as time keeps passing, I'm going for my 2nd gap year now. I want to make use of all the time I have available to prepare myself and fill in my knowledge gaps from high school.

I am ambitious and I have these crazy ideas, but I don't have the crazy knowledge yet. So, when I found out about Talento Digital, which offers free online courses for Dominicans, I wanted go into the space and start learning. It's the push I was looking for, so I took the opportunity.

I was expecting to go at my own pace, but I kept pushing myself and feeling rushed, and since I have little to no experience with programming, I had to put off my initial webapp idea. So, instead, I created a website of my webapp idea. And even so, I kept fighting with the gap between knowledge and ideas, which you'll see right now as I take you through the process of making this website.

# The concept of PilaTask
The app, PilaTask, is meant for Dominican students. Driven by my passion of tools and organization systems, it started with an observation I had while I was in highschool: many students at my school are forgetful, and most of the top students depend or prefer using traditional systems like a notebook. Why is that, and how true is it? It was just a hypothesis with no back up nor evidence, so to get a general idea I did a small survey of 10 people not only from private school but from public schools too. Of course, this is just a small sample and I don't have the means to make a bigger-scale survey, but the diversity helped and hinted to something really interesting. Here's the break down.
## The survey
### Method: 
I sent out a short survey to 10 students I know personally (a mix of close friends and acquaintances). 6 in high school and 4 just started university, from both public and private institutions. 

I asked them about how they currently manage tasks, what problem they encounter while doing them, what current system they use and what frustrates them about it, how they'd feel about a task program with a plant to take care of as a gamification system, and how they would like the usage of IA be. This is of course not true for everyone in the region or country, so this isn't a final answer.
### Results:
- 7 out of 10 rely on plain notebook or agenda, not an app.
- Tasks get delayed for different reasons depending on the person: some genuinely forget (mostly the university students), some procrastinate, and some just get overwhelmed with the amount of tasks piling up (mostly high schoolers).

A few highlighted quotes from the problems they encounter with their current system:
> "Que se me rompa el cuaderno." (That my notebook gets destroyed.) "Que [lo que uso actualmente] no me enganche a usarla." (That [what I currently use] doesn't hook me into actually using it.) "Me frustra que se me olvida [la tarea] y no tengo nada que me lo recuerde con antelación." (It frustrates me that I forget [the task] and have nothing that reminds me ahead of time.)

Something interesting of the results was when I asked about the gamification idea. 5 of the 6 high schoolers said "yes, I love achievement systems," while all 4 university students said "maybe, it depends on how easy it is to use."

But the cherry on top was the question about AI usage in a tool like this: nobody asked AI to solve their tasks. Everyone asked it as an assistant for reminders, organization and explanation. While two persons were explicit against AI being the focal point of the app.

## The features
Everything in the Visión section of the site traces back to something form the survey or from my own experience:
- Reminders with advance notice: It was the #1 frustration expressed in the survey.
- Categorization by subject and teacher: bone of the organization system I was planning, and the survey confirmed this.
- A note-scanning feature: aimed for the notebook situation. I believe the app should be a bridge between traditional and digital note-taking, so I came up with the idea of a tool that allows them to capture what they already wrote with the intention to have an easier access to them and to implement a semantic organization system. Fair to say that is a very complex feature to make, but I know this is possible at a certain level.
- AI assistant: I figured that the best usage for AI in the app is for reminders, smart organization, and explanation. I'm keeping it on as a support for students, both in the app's design and in how I plan to talk about it.
- The garden/plant gamification system: It's the actual concept of PilaTask, and I'm keeping it simple and comfortable for most users, but really intentional with the creole and tropical representation.

Before the survey, I already had the basic idea: task and time management system paired with a simple gamification system. Why? It was an idea I grew obsessed with, and left me space to ideate more in the future. 

I've always figured that PilaTask should feel comfortable and modern. I want the visual aspects to be of high level fidelity while representing creole and tropical elements. That cultural angle is the only complexity I'll allow the gamification system have, where users grow their own plant and see the traditional methods or elements proper of our culture based on how well they're keeping up with their tasks.

### Prototyping
Now, you are about to see my first big problem take shape.

I wanted scrolling behaviors in my website to mimic modern ones (like the ones you see created by Squarespace, for example) because I wanted a "wow" factor and interactivity. I specifically wanted to make some elements fixed on certain sections of the page, especially to the orange to make it look as if it was falling. 

I wanted to try Figma for prototyping, but it was challenging. I've never used it before, and even if I watched tutorials, I couldn't get what I wanted. I got most of the scrolling animations ideas from this amazing website: [kosa](https://ilovecreatives.com/internet-gem-websites/kosa)

After a whole week of struggle, I only got to create some of the assets you see in the website, plus the two first sections. The problem was that it was taking me a lot of time and energy, and I didn't achieve a full scrollable website with some sections having fixed elements coming in (as in the kosa website above).

I decided to simplify things by taking a step back and using the only tool that could fix my problem: Canva. I have a lot of experience with Canva, and I realized that I shouldn't complicate things for myself by learning a new tool. I'm not saying that I'm giving up on Figma, I just wasn't in the right headspace for it. Along the way, I also came across with Rive, but I didn't have the time to fully learn it if I wanted to finish this in a month.

I finally got my ideas flowing, which is where you'll see the closest look to the current website. Although this isn't a proper prototype itself, it worked.

### CSS
Now, it's finally time for the "fun" part.

As someone used to Canva, or the simple "click and drag" tool, learning CSS was the most challenging part of everything. The principal culprit was an animated orange I wanted to implement. I couldn't learn Rive, so I intended to make the orange fixed on the screen, but everything kept breaking and overcomplicating.  

I was on my breaking point, so I decided to skip all those ideas.  At this point, I just wanted something to look okay on my website, and I couldn't get there because I was missing a crucial skill:  flexbox and grids. As a visual learner, I searched videos that helped me understand them, and applied it to my website successfully. This was when I finally saw everything coming together. 

And for the sake of the process, I used what I learned to write media queries for phone devices.

Thenm I used Javascript to add the hamburger menu and form validation. These where simple interactive features, so they weren't difficult. I just came back to tutorial hunting.

I didn't touch on backend since I didn't feel it necessary for this website. what I'm using is Google Sheets to gather data from anyone who fills out the form. It's simple and gets the job done.

# Observations and improvement areas
If I were to do this again, and knowing all I went through... I wouldn't say I wouldn't do it because at some point it was worth it, but at what cost? 

It was the fact that I'm still a beginner and it feels like I'm overplanning and compromising on stuff that I don't even know if I'll be able to do or achieve.

Let's take the scanning feature as an example. I'm not exactly saying that it'll detect the user's handwritten notes because it'll involve a large neural network and a lot of training. Of course, this is possible, but is about how far can I go. Now imagine this with the other bunch of features and small interactions I want on my app.

At least, what I know how to do know is the process of making a website, from wireframe to product. I now know how to use HTML, CSS and a bit of JavaScript to achieve what I did. But to make more complex stuff like user login, task system, and more, it'll take a while. This is what it surprised me the most: as someone who only consumes technology, I learned a lot in a single month.

I'm just starting out in the world of programming, but I would like to take a step at a time because even if I made a lot of progress, I need to process it.

This is exactly what I'm planning to do. I want to apply all the knowledge I got from this experience to small and fun personal projects, and actually learn more along the way. I actually didn't touch on backend territory, so I'm hoping to come back to it soon. 

I still have many plans for this project and I see its potential, but I need to make strides. If I have to take more than a step back to achieve it, I'll do it.
